Residency

NG Art Residency
Provence, France

Four weeks in Maussane-les-Alpilles. Painting from life, painting from memory, and learning to tell the difference between the two.

Date
September 2024 Location
Maussane-les-Alpilles, Provence Duration
4 weeks Medium
Oil, plein air + studio

Why Provence

I applied to the NG Art Residency without expecting to get in. I had been making work consistently — the La Roane retreat in France the previous year had cracked something open — but a full month in Provence felt like a different scale of commitment. To the practice, to myself, to the idea that this was real and not a hobby I was managing around the rest of my life.

I got in. I went. It changed things.

The Work

Provence in September is specific. The light is late-summer thick, gold by 4pm, gone fast. The olive groves and the limestone hills don't look like a painting — they look like they've always been painted, which makes it harder, not easier. You're working against everything you've already seen.

I painted en plein air every morning and worked in studio every afternoon. The plein air work was about learning to see without filtering. The studio work was where I started understanding what I actually remembered — which is different from what I saw.

The interesting paintings weren't the ones that looked like Provence. They were the ones that felt like what it was like to be in Provence — the heat, the stillness, the particular quality of doing nothing important somewhere very beautiful.

By week three I had stopped trying to capture the landscape and started using it as raw material for something more interior. That shift is visible in the work.

Timeline

Week 1
Arrival. Orientation. Plein air in the village and surrounding hills. Mostly bad paintings. Necessary.
Week 2
Found a rhythm. Morning light, olive groves, late afternoon in studio. First good canvas.
Week 3
The shift. Less observational, more interior. Started working larger. This is where the real work happened.
Week 4
Finishing, editing, letting go of pieces that weren't ready. Documented everything. Didn't want to leave.

What It Changed

The residency made the practice non-negotiable. When you spend four weeks doing nothing but making work — no meetings, no client deliverables, no context-switching — you stop being able to pretend it's optional. That's both freeing and inconvenient.

Work from this period was shown at The Dominion in San Antonio (March 2026). Some of it sold. Some of it is still in my studio. Some of it I painted over.
