What It Was

I started the newsletter in 2020, during the first year of working at TINT. The industry was moving fast — social platforms fragmenting, creator economy emerging, UGC shifting from nice-to-have to strategic — and I was reading everything I could find, synthesizing it, and sharing it internally. At some point someone said: this should be public.

So I made it public. I wrote it every week for three years without missing an issue. That discipline taught me more about communication than anything else I've done professionally.

The constraint of publishing on a schedule — regardless of whether you feel ready, inspired, or smart enough — forces a kind of clarity that's impossible to develop any other way.

What It Covered

The newsletter sat at the intersection of brand strategy, consumer behavior, and platform dynamics. Each issue took one thread — a platform shift, a cultural moment, a brand decision — and pulled it until something useful came out.

Topics over the three years included: the maturation of influencer marketing, the brand implications of Web3 (and its collapse), the rise of community-led growth, the creator economy's structural problems, AI entering creative work, and what authenticity actually means as a brand signal versus a marketing word.

What I Learned

Writing to 50,000 people weekly for three years is a forcing function for intellectual honesty. You can't hedge. You can't say "it depends" and send that as a newsletter. You have to have a point of view, defend it, and then update it publicly when you're wrong.

That practice — of committing to a position, publishing it, and being accountable to it — shapes how I think about brand strategy now. A brand that doesn't have a point of view isn't a brand. It's a logo.

The newsletter ended in 2023 when I shifted focus to building Whitespace. Some of what I wrote there became the intellectual foundation for how Whitespace approaches positioning and narrative work.
